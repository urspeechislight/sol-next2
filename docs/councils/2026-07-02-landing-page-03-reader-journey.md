# Council submission — reader journey lens (2026-07-02)

## Diagnosis: a widget board wearing a landing page's clothes

First-ever visit: the page never answers "what is this and how serious is
it"; the corpus's scale, its single most credibility-carrying fact, appears
nowhere. Daily return visit: four equal cards force a scan-and-choose every
morning; verse and hadith are nourishment, chronicles and calendar are
trivia, and the layout refuses to say so; the richest material (tafsīr,
grading, parallels) is buried at card depth. Task-driven visit: nothing; a
reader 300 pages into a work has no path back, and search exists only as
header chrome. The emotional register missed is the threshold of a reading
room, where today's reading has been laid open on the lectern.

## Journey design, ranked by impact

1. Continue reading, top slot, conditional on a stored position: the
   cheapest win on the list.
2. One lectern, not four cards: the verse full-width and first, Arabic
   leading, with tafsīr and "read in context" wiring devotion into study;
   the hadith second at reduced weight with its grading and parallels;
   chronicles and calendar below the fold as one quiet strip.
3. Name the library above the fold: scale plus domain names as live links;
   the first-timer's single primary action is Browse.
4. Below the fold: book of the day with its rationale, the almanac, and a
   body-level search presence.

## Retention without gamification

Rhythm, not rewards: the Hijri date as the page's clock; continuity through
the resume bar and recent reads; no streaks on a sacred corpus.

## References

Wikipedia Main Page (one featured item dominates, sections know their
place), Poets.org Poem-a-Day (full text as the page, not a teaser), and
Merriam-Webster Word of the Day (layered depth teaching readers to scroll).
